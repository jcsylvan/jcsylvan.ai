Export License Board

Every export authorization this business holds, laid out as a board of hex tiles — one tile per destination country. The colour of a tile is the state of that country's licenses, the chip in the middle counts the live authorizations, and clicking a tile opens the detail: what each license covers, who the end-user is, how much of the authorized value is drawn down, when it lapses, and which conditions ride on it. The calendar under the board turns all of that into a work queue.

Proof-of-concept demo. The portfolio belongs to a fictional exporter (“Helios Dynamics”). Every license number, end-user, date, dollar figure and condition is invented, and tile placement is schematic rather than geographic. Anything you add or tick on the board is kept in this browser only.

The chip on a tile counts the authorizations that can actually carry a shipment today. The pips under the number show how far the most drawn-down of those licenses has run: one pip is barely touched, five is nearly exhausted. A tile with no live authorization carries a marker instead — a circled arrow for an application still out, a dash for nothing on file, and a cross for a restricted destination. An amber dot in the corner means a condition falls due soon, and a red one means a condition is already overdue. The three harbours mark the AUKUS partners, where much defence trade moves under the §126.7 exemption rather than a license.

Not legal or compliance advice. This is a tracking aid built on invented data, not a system of record. A real license portfolio lives in your export-management system, and the authoritative state of any authorization is what BIS (SNAP-R), DDTC (DECCS) or the issuing agency shows — including conditions, provisos, quantity limits and reporting obligations beyond the few modelled here. Dates are measured against the portfolio date above rather than today's date, so the demo stays readable over time. Screening an item for classification is a separate step — see the Export License Screener, which reads this same file to tell you whether what you are screening is already covered.
