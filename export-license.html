Export License Board

Every export authorization this business holds, laid out as a board of hex tiles — one tile per destination country. The colour of a tile is the state of that country's licenses, the chip in the middle counts the live authorizations, and clicking a tile opens the detail: what each license covers, who the end-user is, how much of the authorized value is drawn down, and when it lapses.

Proof-of-concept demo. The portfolio belongs to a fictional exporter (“Helios Dynamics”). Every license number, end-user, date and dollar figure is invented, and tile placement is schematic rather than geographic. Anything you add or change on the board is kept in this browser only.

The chip on a tile counts the authorizations that can actually carry a shipment today. The pips under the number show how far the most drawn-down of those licenses has run: one pip is barely touched, five is nearly exhausted. A tile with no live authorization carries a marker instead — a circled arrow for an application still out, a dash for nothing on file, and a cross for a restricted destination.

Not legal or compliance advice. This is a tracking aid built on invented data, not a system of record. A real license portfolio lives in your export-management system, and the authoritative state of any authorization is what BIS (SNAP-R), DDTC (DECCS) or the issuing agency shows — including conditions, provisos, quantity limits and reporting obligations that a tile cannot capture. Expiry dates here are measured against the portfolio date above rather than today's date, so the demo stays readable over time. Screening an item for classification is a separate step — see the Export License Screener. Edit export-licenses.json to change the board.
